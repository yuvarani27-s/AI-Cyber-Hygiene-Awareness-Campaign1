Essential Cyber Hygiene for Students

Cyber hygiene isn't rocket science; it's a collection of consistent digital habits that protect 99% of student accounts from compromise.

Core Foundation

Passphrases Over Short Passwords

Replace single complex words with long 4-word passphrases like Falcon#Coffee$Library!99. Length defeats brute-force attacks exponential times faster than complexity.

Risk: A 7-character password is cracked in milliseconds by modern GPU clusters.

Password reuse across gaming sites, student forums, and campus portals is the #1 vector for "credential stuffing". When one minor forum leaks, attackers script automated logins across your Google, GitHub, and college accounts.

Uncompromising Barrier

Hardware / App-Based 2FA

Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) everywhere. Prefer Authenticator Apps (Google Authenticator, Aegis, Bitwarden) over SMS OTPs which are susceptible to SIM swapping.

Risk: SMS OTPs can be intercepted via telecom SS7 flaws or social-engineered SIM swaps.

Beware of "MFA Fatigue" or "Prompt Bombing": if you suddenly receive 10 notification popups asking to approve login at 3 AM, do NOT tap approve to stop the alerts! Change your password immediately.

Network Defense

Public & Campus Wi-Fi Hygiene

Unencrypted Wi-Fi in cafes, libraries, and airports allows rogue access points ("Evil Twin" hotspots) to snoop traffic. Turn off auto-connect and always ensure HTTPS.

Risk: Man-In-The-Middle (MITM) attackers harvest unencrypted cookies, session tokens, and credentials.

Rogue actors set up small portable devices broadcasting "College_Free_WiFi". Any student whose laptop is set to auto-connect joins the rogue hotspot, routing all unencrypted traffic through the attacker's analyzer.

System Hardening

Zero-Day & Patch Management

"Remind me tomorrow" is an invitation to malware. Operating system, browser, and smartphone updates patch publicly known zero-day exploits actively abused by malware kits.

Risk: Unpatched Chromium and WebKit flaws allow remote drive-by malware execution from malicious ads.

Student computers are often compromised via "game cracks" or "free textbook PDF" download sites bundled with silent infostealer malware (like RedLine, Lumma). Infostealers silently copy all your saved browser cookies and session keys.

Privacy Shield

Digital Footprint & Anti-Doxxing

Posting photos of your college ID card, exam admit card, travel flight tickets, or hostel room leaks sensitive metadata (barcode, roll numbers, DOB) used for social engineering.

Risk: Attackers use your public birthday and mother's maiden name to answer bank security questions.

Boarding passes, concert tickets, and campus ID cards contain 2D barcodes containing your full legal name, government identification, and account reference numbers. Scammers decode them directly from high-resolution Instagram posts.

AI Safety

AI Voice Clones & Prompt Privacy

Generative AI enables convincing voice clones and synthetic phishing. Never paste API keys, confidential assignments, or personal IDs into public AI prompt windows.

Risk: Scammers clone a student's voice to panic parents into sending emergency money.

If your family receives an urgent phone call claiming you've been arrested or hospitalized and requesting immediate UPI transfer, urge them to pause: call your phone directly, or verify with campus warden before transferring any funds.

AI Threat Spotlight

Generative AI Voice Scams

Scammers capture 5 seconds of your speech from reels, clone your voice using generative models, and phone your parents pretending you need emergency cash.

Countermeasure: Establish an offline family passphrase that an AI clone could never guess.
Campus QR Traps

Beware of "Quishing" (QR Phishing)

Malicious QR stickers pasted over legitimate cafeteria menus or campus notice boards direct students to credential-harvesting phishing portals or malicious APK downloads.

Countermeasure: Always preview the decoded destination URL before confirming browser redirection.
Piracy Danger

Cracked Software & Free Game Mods

Over 70% of cracked software, torrent game keygens, and cheat engines contain silent Infostealers (e.g. Lumma, RedLine) that exfiltrate saved passwords and browser cookies.

Countermeasure: Leverage free student GitHub packs, open-source alternatives, or authorized educational licenses.
Job & Internship Fraud

Work-From-Home & Telegram Scams

Messages promising ₹3,000/day for liking YouTube videos or typing assignments are advance-fee pyramid schemes. They eventually ask you to deposit money to "unlock" earnings.

Countermeasure: Legitimate companies will NEVER ask students to pay for onboarding or tasks.

Toll-free national helpline dedicated to freezing fraudulent financial transactions (UPI, credit cards, netbanking) in real time.

The Golden Hour Rule: Calling 1930 within 2 hours of unauthorized debit allows law enforcement to freeze money in the beneficiary account before withdrawal.

Which Reporting Option Should Students Use?

Financial Scams

UPI Fraud / Debit OTP / Card Phishing

Action Protocol:
  1. Step 1: Call 1930 immediately to initiate payment freeze.
  2. Step 2: Call your bank customer care to block your card and UPI ID.
  3. Step 3: File a detailed incident report on cybercrime.gov.in.
Harassment & Safety

Cyberbullying / Deepfake Defamation / Sextortion

Action Protocol:
  1. Step 1: Do not pay blackmailers; block communication immediately.
  2. Step 2: Report on cybercrime.gov.in under "Report Crime Against Women & Children" (can report anonymously).
  3. Step 3: Report content directly to the platform's Resident Grievance Officer in India.
Account Takeover

Hacked Instagram / Google / College Portal

Action Protocol:
  1. Step 1: Use official account recovery flows via registered emergency email.
  2. Step 2: Inform friends on other channels so they ignore impersonation messages.
  3. Step 3: File an identity theft complaint at the local Cyber Police Station if recovery fails.
Malicious Sites

Phishing Domains & Fake University Portals

Action Protocol:
  1. Step 1: Forward the phishing email or link to incident@cert-in.org.in (CERT-In).
  2. Step 2: Report the phishing URL to Google Safe Browsing and Microsoft Defender for takedown.
  3. Step 3: Alert your college IT department if a campus portal is impersonated.

Critical Evidence to Preserve Before Reporting

Police and cyber cells require concrete digital evidence. Never delete messages or wipe your phone before documenting:

✔ Transaction Reference: UTR number, UPI transaction ID, bank statement copy.
✔ Screenshots: Full uncropped screenshots showing the scammer's username, phone number, and timestamp.
✔ Email Headers: Full `.eml` or RFC 822 original message headers showing the true sender IP.
✔ Voice / Call Records: Audio recordings of extortion calls and the caller's phone numbers.
